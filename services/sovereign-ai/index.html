It starts here
“None of our data can leave the country.”
Head of risk, financial services
  1. Week 1We ask what the requirement actually is. It turns out to be three requirements, held by three teams, that do not agree with one another. Residency was the word everyone used; retention and key custody were what they each meant.
  2. Week 2The rule is written down and signed off — where data may rest, where it may be processed, how long anything is kept, who holds the keys. One page. Every later decision gets checked against it.
  3. Weeks 3–4Deployment options costed honestly: on-premises, private cloud in-region, hybrid with a boundary — including the option where the answer is a managed service under the right contract, because sometimes it is.
  4. Weeks 5–8Built inside the constraint rather than adapted to it. Every call carries an audit record: what was sent, which model, where it ran, what came back.
  5. Week 9We sit with an internal auditor and try to answer their questions from the logs alone. Two cannot be answered. Both are fixed before anyone calls it done.
What we need from you The engagement stalls without these
  • The actual rule, not the summaryThe clause, the regulator, the contract. “It cannot leave the country” is a starting point, not a requirement.
  • Someone who can decideLegal, security and infrastructure disagreeing in week eight costs more than any architecture choice made in week three.
  • Honest hardware and budget limitsRunning weights in-region has a floor. We would rather design to the real number than discover it after the architecture is set.
  • The audit you will eventually faceWho asks, and what they accept as evidence. Retrofitting an audit trail is close to rebuilding.
What usually goes wrong And what we do about it

Sovereignty is added at the end.

It is designed for from the first line. Retrofitting it is close to a rebuild, and we will say so before you spend the money rather than after.

The requirement is whatever one team believes it is.

We write it down and get it signed. Three teams usually turn out to hold three different requirements, and finding that out is week one’s job.

The audit trail records that a call happened.

Ours records what was sent, which model, where it ran and what came back — and we test it against questions a real auditor asks.

Nobody planned for replacing the model.

Handover covers the day a model has to be swapped, because it will be, and in-region that is not a configuration change.

Questions we get asked Before the first call ends
Does this mean self-hosting an open-weight model?

Sometimes. Sometimes the answer is a managed service in the right region under the right contract. Which of the two applies is a finding, not an assumption, and it comes out of the first fortnight.

Is a sovereign deployment slower or worse?

Usually somewhat more expensive and somewhat less convenient. Whether it is worse depends entirely on what happens to you if residency turns out to be wrong.

Can you work with our existing infrastructure team?

That is the intended arrangement rather than a concession. They will be operating this afterwards, so they should be building it alongside us.

Is this a service of its own, or a constraint on the others?

Honestly, both — and mostly the second. It shapes an implementation or an agent system rather than replacing one. This page is the workstream that establishes the constraint, designs to it and proves it holds.

What about the DPDP Act, the EU AI Act, or our sector’s rules?

We build to the rule you are actually held to, with your legal team in the room. We do not give legal advice — we take their reading of it and make the system demonstrably match, in a form an auditor will accept.
