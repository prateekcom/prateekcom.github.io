It starts here
“Which model should we be using?”
Director of data, healthcare
  1. Week 1The question becomes testable: not “which model is best”, but “can any of them pull these six fields out of our documents at the accuracy the downstream process needs”. The threshold comes from the process, not from a leaderboard.
  2. Week 2Four hundred documents labelled by hand, including the awkward ones people usually leave out. The set is frozen and kept.
  3. Weeks 3–4Four approaches run, including a boring baseline nobody wanted to test. The baseline comes second, at a fraction of the cost — and it is the finding that changes the plan.
  4. Week 5Failure modes named. The strongest model fails on one document layout that happens to be nine per cent of the corpus, and rising.
  5. Week 6A recommendation with an expiry: what would have to change for it to stop being true, and the evaluation set handed over so it can be re-run without us.
What we need from you The engagement stalls without these
  • Your data, not a public benchmarkA result measured on somebody else’s corpus is a conversation piece. It cannot carry a decision.
  • A threshold from the processWhat accuracy is genuinely good enough, and who says so. Without it, “better” is unfalsifiable.
  • Time from someone who can labelA few hundred examples judged by a person who knows the domain. It is the single highest-value input to the whole exercise.
  • Permission to report a negativeSometimes no approach clears the bar. That has to be an acceptable answer, or the exercise is theatre.
What usually goes wrong And what we do about it

It is benchmarked on a public set.

We evaluate on your task and your data. Public benchmarks are useful for choosing what to test and useless for deciding.

There is no baseline.

The boring approach is always in the comparison. It wins often enough that leaving it out is close to negligent.

Only the headline number is reported.

Failure modes are named and quantified. An average hides the nine per cent that will generate every complaint you receive.

The finding has no expiry.

Every recommendation states what would have to change for it to stop holding, and the evaluation set is handed over so you can check.

Questions we get asked Before the first call ends
How is this different from a proof of concept?

A proof of concept shows something can work once. This measures how well, on your data, against the alternatives, with the failure modes named. A demo is not evidence, and boards have started noticing.

Can you compare vendors for us?

Yes — on your task, measuring the same thing for each. That is usually the reason the vendor claims could not be compared in the first place.

What if the result is negative?

You get it, with the evidence behind it. Establishing that an approach does not clear the bar is an inexpensive way to avoid a build that was never going to.

Do we keep the evaluation set?

Always. It is the most durable thing the engagement produces, and it is what lets you re-run the comparison yourself when a new model appears.

How long is a finding good for?

We put an expiry on it. Six months is common for anything resting on a frontier model; considerably longer for findings about the shape of your own data.
